Installing a water heater is a planned job when you are adding a unit, moving it, or upgrading before the old one fails. For homeowners in Cupertino, CA a good installation starts with the right size for your household, the right fuel for your service, and a location that meets clearance and venting rules. We schedule installs, and the 24/7 line stays open for questions.

A complete installation covers the unit, water connections, shutoff valves, an expansion tank where needed, the relief valve and discharge pipe, venting or condensate drain, a drain pan where required, and earthquake strapping.

Sizing and location

Household size, number of bathrooms and peak demand set the tank size. A family that runs the dishwasher, a shower and laundry at once needs more capacity than a couple who showers at different times. Tankless units are sized by flow, in gallons per minute, and by temperature rise.

Location matters just as much. Gas tank units need combustion air and a vent path. Heat pump units need open air space around them and a place to send condensate. An attic or a closet may not meet those needs.

The parts of the job most people miss

Expansion tank

A closed water system needs one to absorb thermal expansion and protect fittings.

Seismic straps

California requires bracing, anchoring or strapping, and inspectors check it.

Relief valve discharge

A properly sized pipe runs to a safe termination, never capped.

Gas shutoff and sediment trap

A dedicated shutoff and a drip leg or sediment trap protect gas units.

Permits and the Cupertino process

A new installation or a move of a water heater is plumbing work that requires a permit in Cupertino. The instant online permit the city offers covers a standard gas tank replacement with conditions, while a new location, a tankless unit or a heat pump goes through the standard process. We prepare the paperwork and schedule the inspection.

The city also notes that a final inspection cannot be scheduled until the contractor and subcontractors hold a City of Cupertino business license. Ask any plumber you hire about it, and check the license on the California Contractors State License Board site.
