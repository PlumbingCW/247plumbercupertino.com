Drain cleaning means removing a blockage so water flows again, then working out why it formed. A sink or tub usually takes a cable or hand auger. A main line may need a sectional machine or hydro jetting. A clog that keeps returning deserves a look with a sewer camera before anyone clears it a third time.

Kitchen sink clogs, the most common call

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe and coats the walls, food scraps catch on that film, and within months a three-inch line behaves like a one-inch line. Homes with a dishwasher draining into the same trap, or a garbage disposal feeding it, show symptoms first because they push the most debris through.

A slow sink that drains after a few minutes usually has a partial blockage near the trap or the first few feet of the branch line. A sink that stays full and gurgles when the dishwasher runs usually has a blockage farther down. We start at the closest cleanout or trap, run a cable that matches the pipe size, and flush the line with hot water so you can see it run clear before we leave.

Signs the clog is in the main line

One slow fixture points to a branch clog. Several slow fixtures point to the main line, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them.

More than one fixture is slow

A tub and a toilet that both drain slowly share a line, and the clog sits downstream of both.

Gurgling in a drain you did not use

Air pushed out of a blocked line escapes through the nearest trap, often a shower or floor drain.

Water rises in the tub after a wash cycle

The washing machine drain empties into the main line, so the lowest fixture shows the backup first.

A sewage smell near the cleanout

A cap in the yard or garage that smells or weeps points to a blockage in the lateral.

When a drain clog is an emergency

Treat it as an emergency in three cases. Wastewater is coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet. The only bathroom in the house is unusable. Or the water is heading for flooring or a finished basement. Shut off water to the house if you cannot stop an overflowing fixture, and keep children and pets out of the area.

What not to do while waiting

Do not run the dishwasher, shower or laundry. Every gallon you add goes to the lowest open drain in the house. Do not plunge a toilet that is backing up from the main line, because pressure can force wastewater back out of the floor drain.

Tub, shower and floor drain notes

Hair and soap scum build a plug in the first foot of a shower drain, and a hand-held drain tool often clears it. If the shower clears but returns within weeks, the problem is usually deeper in the branch line or at the connection to the stack. A floor drain in a garage or laundry room that backs up is a different story, because it connects to the main line and tells you the blockage is downstream.

A drain line that leaks is a separate repair from a clog. Wet drywall below a bathroom, a musty cabinet, or a stain that grows after every shower can mean a cracked or loose drain joint, and clearing the line will not fix it. We check for that before we recommend anything beyond a cleaning.

Mature landscaping around Stevens Creek Boulevard and the older streets near Monta Vista puts roots near sewer laterals, and roots find the tiny gaps at pipe joints. A cable can cut them back, but the roots return unless the joint is sealed or the pipe is lined or replaced. Ask your sanitary district where the public main begins, because the homeowner owns the lateral from the house to that connection.

Cupertino is served by more than one sanitary provider, so the party you call about the main depends on your street. The city lists the providers and offers a district map on its utilities page, and a plumber can read your cleanout location and tell you which side of the property line a problem sits on.
