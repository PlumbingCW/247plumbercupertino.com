A sewer camera inspection is a planned look inside the lateral using a waterproof camera head on a push cable. For homeowners in Cupertino, CA it answers three questions: is the pipe intact, where is the damage if there is any, and what does it need. We schedule these visits, and the 24/7 line is open if a repeat clog makes it urgent.

The camera runs from the cleanout to the public connection, records video, and tracks the distance to anything unusual. A locator at the surface marks the spot so a repair crew can dig where it counts.

Three reasons people book a camera pass

Buying a home

Pipe condition is invisible at a showing, and a defective lateral can be a major repair after closing.

Repeat clogs

A third backup means something physical is holding debris, and the camera shows what.

Before a remodel or landscaping

Knowing where the pipe runs, and how deep, keeps trenchers and tree roots away from it.

After a repair

A second pass confirms the work and records the new condition for your files.

What the camera finds and what it means

FindingWhat it usually meansTypical next step
Roots at a joint.Gap in the joint letting roots in.Cut roots, then seal or line.
Offset or sag.Soil movement or a settled pipe.Spot repair or replacement of the section.
Scale or grease coating.Buildup narrowing the bore.Hydro jetting.
Cracked or broken pipe.Age, soil load or impact.Repair, lining or replacement.
Clean, sound pipe.The clog was a one-time item.Routine care.

How the inspection visit works

  1. 1

    Locate the cleanout.

    We open the access point or pull a fixture if no cleanout exists.

  2. 2

    Clear if needed.

    A line packed with debris is cleaned enough for the camera to pass.

  3. 3

    Run and record.

    The camera travels the lateral to the connection, with video and distance noted.

  4. 4

    Review together.

    We show the footage and explain what each finding means for cost and timing.

Buying a home in Cupertino

A home inspection usually does not include the underground lateral. A buyer who adds a camera pass learns the pipe condition before the contingency period ends, and a seller who has one on hand can answer questions faster. Ask for the video file and for a note of the distance to each defect.

The footage does not replace a license or a permit check, but it gives you a real number to negotiate with. Cupertino buyers often tour homes in the neighborhoods around Monta Vista and Rancho Rinconada, and a lateral that has served a long time is the question worth asking.

What you get after the inspection

You receive the video, the notes on distance and depth, and an honest recommendation. If the pipe is sound, we say so and you pay for the inspection only. If the pipe needs work, we explain the options and quote them as separate items, so you can compare. See sewer line repair for how those options are chosen.

Repairs that follow an inspection fall under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and many need a permit.
