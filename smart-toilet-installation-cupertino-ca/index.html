Smart Toilet Installation in Cupertino, CA: What to Check First

A smart toilet is more than a fixture swap. It needs power near the toilet, a good water connection and a sound flange. Check these first and the install goes smoothly.

By the Cupertino 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

Smart toilets, with heated seats, bidet functions and sometimes automatic lids, have become a common request in Cupertino remodels. They look like a normal toilet, but they need electricity and a clean water supply, and some models need a tank or a pressure minimum. A little planning before you buy saves a second trip to the hardware store.

This post covers what to check, what a plumber and an electrician each handle, and where a standard toilet repair visit ends and a remodel project begins.

Decide between a full smart toilet and a smart seat

A full smart toilet replaces the whole fixture. It is usually a one piece or a skirted design with the electronics built in. A smart seat is a bidet seat that replaces your existing seat and keeps your toilet. The seat is simpler and often cheaper. The full unit looks cleaner and can be more efficient with water.

If your current toilet works well and the bowl shape fits, a seat may be all you need. If you are already replacing an old toilet, a full unit is worth considering.

Power near the toilet

Smart toilets need a grounded outlet within reach of the cord. In most bathrooms the outlet has to be a ground fault protected one, because it sits near water. If there is no outlet near the toilet, an electrician needs to add one. Extension cords are not a safe fix.

Plan this before you buy. The cord length, the location of the outlet and the position of the water line all affect which model fits your bathroom.

Do not skip this

Never plug a smart toilet into an outlet that is not ground fault protected near water. If you are not sure what is behind the wall, ask an electrician to check before the plumber sets the fixture.

Water supply and pressure

Many smart toilets need a minimum water pressure to flush and run the bidet. The spec sheet lists it. If your home has low pressure, a pressure assisted unit or a tank model may fit better. A plumber can test your pressure in a few minutes.

The supply line also matters. Old stop valves that stick or leak should be replaced during the install. A new braided line and a working shutoff cost little compared with a flood.

Rough in and flange condition

Standard toilets use a rough in distance from the wall to the center of the drain, and smart toilets are made for specific distances. Measure yours before you choose a model. Most homes use a common distance, but older bathrooms can differ.

The flange is the ring that the toilet bolts to. If it is cracked, rusted or sits below the finished floor, the toilet will rock and leak at the base. A plumber can repair or replace the flange before the new unit goes on.

Weight, floors and access

A full smart toilet is heavy, and it often arrives in a large box. Plan the path from your door to the bathroom and have a second person for the lift. Protect the floor, and check that the subfloor is sound, because a soft spot near a leaking old toilet needs repair first.

Installation steps in plain terms

  1. 1

    Shut off water.

    Close the stop valve, flush to empty the tank and sponge out what is left.

  2. 2

    Remove the old toilet.

    Disconnect the supply, remove the bolts, lift the toilet and cover the open drain.

  3. 3

    Check the flange.

    Clean the flange, look for cracks and level, and set a new wax or rubber seal.

  4. 4

    Set the new unit.

    Lower the toilet straight down, press evenly and tighten the bolts a little at a time.

  5. 5

    Connect and test.

    Connect the supply, plug in the power, flush several times and check every joint for leaks.

Permits and older Cupertino bathrooms

A simple toilet swap normally does not need a permit, but adding an outlet or moving a drain can. Ask the City of Cupertino Building Division what applies to your project, and ask your electrician to pull any electrical permit that is needed. A remodel that moves plumbing is a bigger job with its own rules.

Homes in Cupertino range from older tract houses to newer construction, and the bathroom shell can differ a lot. Do not assume the rough in distance or the wall wiring matches the box. Measure first. If you are also fixing a leak around the base, a leak check before the remodel is a good idea.
