Replacing a Water Heater Gas Control Valve in Cupertino, CA

The gas control valve is the brain of a gas water heater. When it fails, you may lose hot water, or the pilot may not stay lit. Here is how to tell, and who should do the work.

By the Cupertino 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026. Updated November 16, 2026.

The gas control valve sits at the base of a gas tank water heater. It holds the thermostat, the pilot controls and the main gas valve. When it fails, the heater can stop heating, the pilot can go out or the burner can behave erratically.

Because it controls gas, this is not a casual fix. Most homeowners should leave it to a licensed plumber, and our water heater repair service covers it. This guide explains what is involved so you know what to ask.

Signs of a failing gas control valve

Pilot will not stay lit

Even after a clean thermocouple, the pilot keeps going out.

No hot water

The burner does not fire when the thermostat calls for heat.

Water too hot or too cold

The thermostat inside the valve no longer regulates temperature.

Burner will not shut off

The flame stays on past the set temperature, which is a safety issue.

Clicking or no response

The valve controls do not respond when you turn the dial.

Check the simple things first

Before you blame the valve, check that the gas is on, the pilot is lit and the thermocouple is clean and positioned in the flame. A worn thermocouple is a much cheaper part and often the real cause of a pilot that keeps going out.

Check the venting too. A blocked flue can shut the heater down for safety. If you do not feel confident with a gas appliance, stop and call.

If you smell gas

Leave the building right away. Do not turn on lights or use a switch. From outside, call the gas utility or 911. Do not go back in until you are told it is safe.

What replacement involves

  1. 1

    Shut off the gas and water.

    Turn the gas to off at the appliance and close the cold water supply to the heater.

  2. 2

    Drain if needed.

    Some valves thread into the tank and need the tank partly drained.

  3. 3

    Disconnect the gas and pilot lines.

    Remove the supply line, the pilot tube and the thermocouple and igniter leads.

  4. 4

    Swap the valve.

    Unscrew the old valve, fit the matching new one with proper thread seal and tighten.

  5. 5

    Reconnect and leak test.

    Reattach the lines, restore gas and test every joint with a gas leak solution before lighting the pilot.

  6. 6

    Relight and verify.

    Light the pilot, set the temperature and watch the burner and the flame for proper operation.

Is a new valve worth it

A control valve on a young heater with a sound tank is usually worth replacing. On an older heater near the end of its life, the labor and the part may not make sense. A tank that is rusting or leaking is not worth putting a new valve on.

Ask the plumber for the age of the heater, the condition of the tank and a clear price for both repair and replacement. See water heater replacement for what a new unit involves.

What a good plumber checks besides the valve

A new valve on a heater with other problems is money wasted. A plumber also checks the flue draft, the burner, the thermocouple, the temperature and pressure relief valve and the tank for rust. They look at the gas line and the connector for age and damage as well.

These checks take a few minutes and often reveal whether a repair makes sense. If the plumber finds a serious problem, you can choose a replacement with the facts in hand.

Safe habits around a gas water heater

Keep the area around the heater clear of boxes, paint and cleaners, since vapors can be flammable. Know where the gas shutoff is, and teach everyone in the home how to find it. If you ever smell gas, leave first and call from outside.

Test the carbon monoxide alarm near the sleeping areas each season, and replace it on the schedule the maker gives.

Gas appliance work is governed by the California codes and the manufacturer instructions, and the plumber works according to both when the valve is replaced.

Gas water heaters and Bay Area rules

Cupertino homes with gas heaters are served by Pacific Gas and Electric gas, so a gas smell is a call to the utility and 911 first. Do not try to trace a leak yourself. A plumber works on the appliance side, and the utility handles its own lines.

Bay Area air district rules limit emissions from new gas water heaters sold for homes, which affects the replacement unit you can buy. A new valve for an existing heater is a repair, but a full replacement unit has to meet the current rules. Replacement is also permitted work, so ask your plumber about the city permit.
