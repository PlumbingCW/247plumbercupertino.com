When a main line backs up and a cable only opens a small channel, hydro jetting is the tool that cleans the pipe itself. Our 24/7 line takes calls for emergency hydro jetting in Cupertino, CA, and the first advice while you wait is the same as for any stoppage: stop using water, keep drains closed, and do not pour chemicals into the line.

Hydro jetting sends water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets forward and backward. The jets strip grease, scale, silt and fine roots off the wall of the pipe, and the backward jets carry the debris out through the cleanout. A good jetting job leaves the pipe bore close to its original size.

Why a cable is not always enough

A cable cuts a hole through a clog. In a line coated with grease or scale, that hole closes again as the new film catches debris, and the homeowner calls back in a few months. Jetting removes the film. For restaurants and for homes with a long run to the main, that difference decides whether the line stays clear.

Callers who describe a line that was cleaned several times in a year are usually describing this pattern. The fix is a different tool, and sometimes a camera visit to see whether a sag, a bad joint or a belly in the pipe is holding water.

When jetting is the wrong call

High pressure water is not for every pipe. Cracked clay, collapsed cast iron, or an offset joint can break further under pressure, and jetting a line that is already failing can push wastewater into the soil. We run a camera through the line before jetting on older pipes, and if it shows damage the plan moves to sewer line repair instead.

Repeat clogs on the same line

Three or more stoppages in a year suggest buildup the cable has not removed.

Slow drains after a recent cleaning

A line that clears and slows within weeks is usually coated, not blocked.

Grease in a kitchen main

Homes with heavy cooking, and any commercial kitchen, build grease faster than residential lines can shed it.

Roots seen on a camera pass

Fine roots at joints are cleared by jetting, but the joint still needs a repair plan.

How a jetting visit runs

  1. 1

    Find the access.

    We locate the cleanout or pull a fixture to reach the line with the hose.

  2. 2

    Camera pass.

    A camera shows pipe material, joints and any damage before pressure goes in.

  3. 3

    Jet the line.

    We choose the nozzle for the debris, work the hose forward, then pull it back with the jets scouring the wall.

  4. 4

    Verify and advise.

    A second camera pass or a flow test confirms the line is clear, and we explain what caused it.

Sewer backup: what to do first

If wastewater is already coming up in a tub or floor drain, shut off the washing machine and avoid flushing. Move belongings away from the drain, and photograph the water line for any insurance claim. Do not run the garbage disposal or dishwasher.

We handle backups at any hour, and the line is jetted once the camera confirms it can take the pressure. Hydro jetting emergencies often involve grease or roots at a joint near the property line, so we note the location for the follow-up repair.

Cupertino conditions that make jetting useful

Homes served by older clay or cast iron laterals collect scale and roots faster than homes with newer plastic pipe. Streets lined with mature trees, like many blocks near De Anza Boulevard and Stevens Creek Boulevard, put roots near joints. Jetting clears the pipe, and a camera finds the joint that let the roots in.
