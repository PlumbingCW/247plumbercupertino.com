Repiping replaces the water supply lines in your house, not the drain lines. For homeowners in Cupertino, CA it is a planned project that is worth a hard look when leaks repeat, water runs brown, or pressure keeps dropping. We schedule repipes, and our 24/7 line is open if a failing line floods in the meantime.

A repipe runs new supply lines from the main shutoff to every fixture, using PEX or copper, and restores the walls afterward. The project can be done in phases, and an experienced crew limits the drywall opened.

Signs your pipes are at the end of their life

Repeated leaks in different spots

A pinhole here and a pinhole there means the whole line is thinning.

Brown or rusty water

Corroding galvanized steel can shed rust into the supply.

Low pressure at every fixture

Scale and corrosion narrow old pipes.

Pipe material that is known to fail

Galvanized steel and polybutylene lines have a history of failure.

Galvanized and polybutylene supply lines

Homes with galvanized steel supply lines see rust and scale narrow the pipe over decades. Polybutylene was widely used in the late twentieth century and has a record of failing at fittings. If your house has either, ask for an inspection, because a failure can be sudden.

If you are not sure what your pipes are made of, a plumber can look at the water meter side, the water heater connections or an access panel. A magnet and a scratch test also help tell galvanized from copper.

Both materials work. PEX is the common choice for whole-house repipes because it is quicker and keeps wall openings small. Copper is a good choice in exposed runs or where you prefer it. We explain which fits your house and why.

Permits and process in Cupertino

Cupertino's Building Division offers an instant online permit for a whole-house repipe, with conditions: interior only, no sewer permits, no historic properties, no HOA, and residential only. Anything outside those conditions goes through a standard permit. Unpermitted work can be stopped, and the city says fees are doubled.

The process starts with an assessment, a written scope and price, a permit, then the work in phases with wall openings kept small. An inspection closes it out. We handle the permit and the schedule.

Repair now or repipe: the decision

Repair is fine when

  • Pipe is sound apart from one failure.
  • The leak was caused by a bad fitting.
  • There is no pattern of pinholes.
  • Water quality is normal.

Repipe is worth discussing when

  • Leaks keep appearing in new spots.
  • Galvanized or polybutylene lines are present.
  • Pressure has dropped everywhere.
  • Rust is showing at faucets.

The cost of repairs adds up. If you have repaired three leaks in a year, the next one is a question of when, not if. We help you compare the cost of ongoing repair against a repipe.

Living through a repipe

Water is off for part of the day, not for days, and we set up temporary water access when needed. Walls are opened in strategic spots and patched, and finish work such as paint is a separate step. Plan for a few days for most houses.

We cover floors and furniture in work areas and clean up each day. We plan the work around your schedule, and we keep you informed.

What affects repipe pricing

House size, number of fixtures, material, access, wall type and permit fees all play in. A single-story house costs less than a two-story one with finished walls. We give a clear price before work starts, in writing.
