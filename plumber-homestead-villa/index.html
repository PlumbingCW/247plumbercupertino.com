Homestead Villa at the northern edge of Cupertino

The City of Cupertino General Plan describes Homestead Villa as the northern edge of the city, in the northwest quadrant of Interstate 280 and Highway 85. Los Altos borders it on the west and north.

The plan lists a mix of single-family homes, clustered homes, townhomes, condominiums and duplexes. It names West Valley Elementary, Cupertino Middle School and Homestead High School as the schools that serve it, and says the area has no public parks or schools inside it.

Plumbing in shared buildings

This section is general plumbing information. In a townhome, condo or duplex, a leak can reach a neighbor within minutes, so speed matters. Shut off the water at the unit valve if there is one, and call your association or property manager as well as a plumber.

Plumbing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, and the City of Cupertino Building Division issues permits. Ask your association what it owns. Many buildings treat the lines inside the walls and the main risers differently from the lines that serve only your unit, and that decides who pays and who calls.

  1. 1

    Find the shutoff.

    Look under sinks, near the water heater or at the utility closet for a valve that serves your unit.

  2. 2

    Call the manager.

    Report the leak to your association or property manager so the building can act on shared lines.

  3. 3

    Call dispatch.

    Say whether water is coming through a ceiling or wall and which floor you are on.

Never put your hand in a disposal. If something is stuck, switch it off and let a plumber clear it. Grease, coffee grounds and fibrous scraps are common causes of kitchen backups in any building. Running cold water for a few seconds after use helps move scraps along.
